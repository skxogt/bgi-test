How We Work

Know what to expect when we work together.

A business question can lead to research, a plan, a website or a defined improvement project. The important part is knowing what is being worked on, what you will receive and what happens next.

BGI Solutions starts with your need. We review the information that matters, agree the scope, do the defined work and bring the outputs back for review. Implementation or continuing support is included only when it is part of your agreement.

Start with the question—not a service name

You can describe a problem, an opportunity or something you need to build. You do not have to know which of our 20 services applies. A single, clearly defined project can be a useful starting point.

You want to understand a problem

Tell us what is happening, what you have tried and which decision you need to make.

You need a practical plan

Explain what you want to change, what you already know and which constraints matter.

You need a website or business material

Share its purpose, intended audience, existing materials and where it will be used.

You have a system to improve

Describe what is running now, what seems unclear and what information is available.

One project, or several connected parts

We discuss the service or combination of services that fits the need. You can start with a website or materials project without first buying an assessment. If additional research, implementation or continuing work is useful, we describe it as a separate scope.

A clear path from first conversation to handover

The stages below describe the proposed project process. The detail can differ depending on whether the work is an assessment, content and design production, or a continuing improvement service.

  1. 01

    Tell us what you need

    Share your business question or project idea, who it should help and what a useful result would look like. We discuss the next information needed to understand the request.

  2. 02

    Review the relevant information

    We identify existing documents, website materials, business records or system requirements that may affect the work. We also note gaps, access needs, sensitive information and assumptions that need checking.

  3. 03

    Agree the project scope

    Before work begins, confirm the intended deliverables, included activities, client inputs, review points, responsibilities, schedule and price. The written scope states what is included and what is outside the project.

  4. 04

    Produce the agreed work

    Depending on the brief, this may mean findings, a roadmap, website content, design files or other agreed assets. We use approved information and bring decisions or uncertainties to the agreed review points.

  5. 05

    Review and check the outputs

    You review the work against the brief and provide feedback through the agreed process. We check the relevant content, files or features to the extent specified in the project. Testing requirements depend on what is being delivered.

  6. 06

    Hand over and decide the next step

    We identify the agreed files or findings, completed checks, open items and any remaining responsibilities. Publishing, technical deployment or ongoing support takes place only where expressly included. You decide whether further work is needed.

What we agree before a project starts

A clear agreement protects the time and expectations of both sides. The details vary by project, but the questions below should be answered before production or assessment begins.

Purpose and outputs

What are we solving, creating or reviewing? Which deliverables will you receive, and in what format? If a recommendation is produced, is carrying it out part of this project or a possible next step?

Inputs and decisions

Who supplies the business facts, materials, access and approvals? Who makes final decisions on content, design, spending or changes to live systems? We identify uncertainties rather than silently turn them into claims.

Price, schedule and review points

What is included in the proposed price, what timetable is realistic for this scope, when will feedback be needed, and what revision or change process applies? No fixed turnaround or price is implied by this page.

Rights, access and third parties

The agreement defines ownership and use of commissioned outputs. Software, stock media, fonts, tools and other third-party materials may have separate licenses. Any required access, storage or handling of customer information is discussed and approved before use.

What is not automatically included

Website hosting, domain purchase, publication, paid advertising, third-party subscriptions, software integration, campaign operation and ongoing maintenance are not assumed. If any are needed, we identify the specific tasks, charges, approvals and owner of each responsibility.

Your part in the process

An identified reviewer, timely feedback and accurate source information help make the review process useful. We agree what you need to supply and when. You should not send sensitive customer records or system credentials until suitable handling arrangements have been confirmed.

Review, quality checks and handover

We agree how the completed work will be reviewed before work begins. An assessment, a business document and a website do not need identical checks.

For research or strategy work

Review the question addressed, evidence used, important assumptions, limits of the findings and decisions the work can support. A recommendation is not the same as a confirmed business result.

For websites and digital materials

Check the approved content, intended page or file structure and specified functionality. Website checks can include navigation, links, mobile layouts, forms, accessibility requirements and the agreed technical environment. The handover records what was actually tested and what remains open.

For ongoing improvement work

Agree the systems, measures, reporting schedule and authority to change anything before reviews begin. The decision record should distinguish observed results from assumptions. Continuing work is never implied by a one-time project.

Working together across time zones

We welcome inquiries from businesses in the United States and other markets. Meeting times, communication channels, review arrangements and response expectations are discussed for each project. This does not imply a US office or guaranteed availability at particular hours.

Two ways a project might start

Illustrative example — A service website

A business has approved service information but needs clearer pages and inquiry steps. A defined project could cover a page plan, copy, responsive layouts and agreed contact-form checks, followed by a recorded handover.

Illustrative example — Understanding an inquiry problem

A business receives inquiries but does not know why few become customers. A defined assessment could review available records, outline plausible causes and explain what needs more evidence before deciding on changes.

These are fictional project descriptions, not accounts of completed client work, typical outcomes or promises of results.

Questions about working with BGI Solutions

Do I have to know which service to choose?

No. Tell us the problem or the materials you need. We can discuss a useful starting point and whether one service or a combined scope fits the work.

Will you give us a fixed price and deadline on this page?

No. Price and schedule depend on the agreed scope, content readiness, complexity and review needs. We discuss them before work starts.

What do you need from our team?

Usually a clear project goal, an authorized decision-maker and available source material. Specific records, access and approval needs are identified before you share sensitive information.

Can you work with an existing website or materials?

We can review what exists and discuss a revision brief. Whether it can be reused depends on the platform, files, permissions and required changes.

How will we review and approve the work?

We agree review points, the person providing feedback, the deliverables to check and the handling of revisions. The scope also defines any tests needed before handover or publication.

Does a project include launch or ongoing support?

Only when those activities are written into the agreement. We distinguish production, handover, launch, hosting and ongoing work, and confirm responsibility for each.
