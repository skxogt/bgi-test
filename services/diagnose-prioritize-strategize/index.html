Diagnose, Prioritize & Strategize

Understand your business and plan growth

Investigate a growth problem, compare opportunities or turn existing findings into a practical plan. BGI Solution helps you understand the evidence, agree priorities and identify what your business needs to carry out the work.

Bring your question, existing findings or current plan. We can discuss a suitable scope for the decision you need to make.

(02)

Services in this family

SVC-01

Find what may be slowing growth

Business Growth Intelligence Assessment & Constraint Diagnosis

Use this service when growth is below expectations and the cause is unclear. We review relevant information, compare possible explanations and investigate the issues that may be holding the business back.

Explore Business Growth Assessment

The agreed deliverables may include

  • Business Growth Intelligence Assessment. A structured review of the situation, information examined and important gaps.
  • Growth Constraint Map. A visual explanation of the main obstacles and how they may connect.
  • Root-Cause Analysis. An explanation of likely causes, alternative explanations and the evidence behind them.
  • Priority Findings Report. A summary of what deserves attention first and why.

The assessment explains the problem and its evidence. Further research, a detailed roadmap and implementation are agreed separately when needed.

SVC-02

Find and prioritize growth opportunities

Growth Opportunity Discovery & Prioritization

Use this service when you need to identify possible growth opportunities or compare ideas already under consideration. We assess their potential value, supporting evidence, feasibility, risks and dependencies before recommending priorities.

Explore Growth Opportunities

An opportunity map records the options and what supports them. A priority matrix compares those options against agreed criteria. Together, they explain which opportunities deserve attention, which assumptions need testing and what could change the priorities.

This work supports a choice about where to focus. Detailed planning or production can be scoped once that choice is clear.

SVC-03

Decide what to do and in what order

Business Growth Strategy & Roadmap Architecture

Use this service when you have relevant findings or accepted priorities and need a practical plan. We work through strategic choices, the sequence of work, resources and dependencies. Existing research or a plan can be reviewed as a starting point.

Explore Growth Strategy and Roadmap

A growth roadmap explains the priorities, order of work and responsibilities. A scorecard identifies the measures and review points used to assess progress. The work records important assumptions and the conditions that could require the plan to change.

The roadmap sets out the work to pursue. Any production, implementation or ongoing management must be stated explicitly in the agreed scope.

SVC-04

Identify what is needed to carry out the plan

Enterprise Capability & Growth System Development

Use this service when a plan needs clearer ownership, stronger processes or suitable technology. We connect the agreed priorities to the capabilities required and identify gaps in the current arrangements.

Explore Capability Development

A capability blueprint shows how people, responsibilities, processes and technology need to work together. A development plan identifies which capability gaps to address, in what order and with which responsibilities.

A growth roadmap sets the priorities; capability development addresses what the business needs to carry them out. The proposal specifies any implementation tasks, who will carry them out and how they will be reviewed.

(03)

Examples of choosing a starting point

The following scenarios illustrate possible project scopes.

You have several ideas but limited resources

A business is considering a new offer, a website revision and a new marketing channel. An opportunity map could record the options; a priority matrix could compare their potential value, evidence and feasibility. The work would support a choice about which opportunity to investigate further.

You have a roadmap but execution is unclear

A business has a roadmap, but responsibilities and processes are unclear. A capability blueprint could identify who owns each part of the work, the processes required and technology gaps. A development plan could then sequence the agreed capability changes.

(04)

How we agree the work

  1. 01

    Define the decision

    Explain the question, the business goal and the decision the work needs to support. We review what is already known before deciding which service fits.

  2. 02

    Agree the scope and information

    Confirm the deliverables, required inputs, responsibilities, price, schedule and review points. Agree how relevant business information will be shared before providing access.

  4. 04

    Review the agreed outputs and their limitations. You choose whether to use them directly or discuss further work under a separate scope.

(05)

What to share when you contact us

Start with your business goal, what is happening now and what you have already tried. Mention any existing research or roadmap and the decision you need to make. We can then discuss which records or access are necessary.

(06)

Questions about these services

How does assessment differ from opportunity prioritization?

Assessment investigates a growth problem and its possible causes. Opportunity discovery and prioritization identifies or compares options for growth. Existing findings can support that comparison; the service you choose depends on the decision and evidence available.

Do I need all four services?

No. Each service addresses a different decision. You can discuss the particular work you need. If another service becomes relevant, we explain its purpose and agree any additional scope before proceeding.

Can you use research or a roadmap we already have?

Tell us what you have, when it was prepared and what decision it addressed. We discuss which material is needed, then review its relevance, evidence and limitations. Any additional validation or research is identified in the scope.

What if our information is incomplete?

We discuss whether the available information can support useful work. Gaps are recorded and findings remain preliminary where evidence is limited. Some questions may need additional research before a sound decision can be made.

Does this include implementing the recommendations?

The proposal identifies which implementation tasks, production work or ongoing management are included and who is responsible. An assessment, roadmap or blueprint may be a standalone deliverable. Any additional work is agreed before it begins.

How are pricing and timing agreed?

They depend on the question, available information, required deliverables and review arrangements. We discuss the defined work, price and expected schedule before you decide to proceed.

Tell us what you need to decide

Share the growth problem, opportunity or plan you want to work through. Tell us what you already know and where you need clarity. We can discuss a suitable starting point and the information needed to define the work.
