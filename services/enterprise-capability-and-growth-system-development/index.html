SVC-04ADiagnose, Prioritize & Strategize

Build the capabilities your growth plan needs.

A good plan needs people who know what to do, processes that support the work and technology that fits the task. BGI Solutions helps you see what your business has in place, what is missing and which capabilities may need attention before the plan can move forward.

We connect accepted business priorities to roles, workflows, tools and decision-making responsibilities. The result is a clearer picture of what your team needs and a practical order for developing it.

Talk to UsExplore All Services

(01)

When this service can help

Your plan is clear, but execution is not

The next steps depend on responsibilities or working arrangements that have not been defined.

Important work falls between teams

Handoffs, approvals or ownership are unclear, causing delays or repeated effort.

Current processes are difficult to follow

The business relies on informal steps that may be inconsistent or hard to review.

You are considering new tools

You need to understand the workflow, information, access and oversight requirements before choosing technology.

(02)

Start with what your business needs to do

Which activities are essential to the agreed plan? Who needs to carry them out and approve changes? Where do current processes or systems get in the way? Which gaps matter now, and which can wait?

You can begin with an existing strategy, project brief or known operating problem. A separate assessment or roadmap project is not mandatory if your starting priorities are already clear.

(03)

How we examine the capabilities behind your plan

We begin with the work your business is trying to carry out, then review the arrangements that support it. The steps below describe a possible approach; your proposal defines the actual activities and depth.

  1. 01

    Confirm the work and its requirements

    Review the accepted goal, selected priorities and activities that need to happen. Identify the key decisions, dependencies and outcomes the capabilities should support. If assumptions about the plan are unresolved, record them before designing a solution around them.

  2. 02

    Review people, processes and technology

    Examine relevant roles, handoffs, repeatable tasks, information flows and current tools. Ask who owns each step, how work is checked and where decisions or approvals happen. Note what already works as well as gaps that could affect delivery.

  3. 03

    Define the needed operating model

    Describe how people, responsibilities, workflows, systems and oversight should fit together for the agreed scope. Distinguish essential requirements from helpful later improvements. Identify constraints, access needs and questions that require input from the people doing the work.

  4. 04

    Prioritize capability changes

    Compare the gaps by importance, practicality, risk and dependency. Propose an order for improving them, with responsible parties, decision points and ways to check readiness. Any detailed system design, setup, training or implementation requires its own agreed scope.

(04)

A useful blueprint starts with real responsibilities

A diagram alone will not make a process work. The recommended arrangement should explain who acts, what they need, how information moves and who makes decisions when a step does not go as planned.

(05)

What you can receive

The work can produce two connected planning outputs. Your proposal confirms which deliverables are included, how detailed they will be and what evidence or stakeholder input they require.

Capability Blueprint

A structured view of the capabilities the business needs to carry out its agreed priorities. The blueprint can map key roles, decision rights, processes, handoffs, information needs and supporting technology. It also identifies gaps between what is required and what is currently in place.

Use it to understand how the parts of a growth system should work together, where responsibilities need clarity and which dependencies could prevent a planned activity from starting.

Capability Development Plan

A prioritized plan for addressing the agreed gaps. It can organize improvements by urgency and readiness, identify owners and prerequisites, and explain which changes should be reviewed before the next stage. A simple maturity or readiness view can show where capabilities are developing, without implying that they have already been implemented.

Use it to decide which capability to strengthen first, what preparation is needed and how the business will know when it is ready for the next step.

(06)

How these outputs work together

The blueprint explains what is needed

It makes the operating relationships, current gaps and intended responsibilities visible.

The development plan explains what to address first

It sequences proposed changes, decisions and readiness checks based on the agreed priorities.

Both are planning deliverables unless the agreement explicitly includes implementation. Recommendations about tools, hiring or process changes remain proposals for the business to assess and approve.

(07)

Illustrative capability-planning situation

This example describes a possible scope. It is not a client case study or a claim that any business improvement has been achieved.

A business has a plan, but inquiries get lost between teams

A service business plans to improve its inquiry process. The website collects interest, but staff members are unsure who reviews an inquiry, who contacts the prospect or how a follow-up is recorded. The business wants a clearer process before adding another tool.

A capability review could map the existing steps and assign proposed responsibilities for receiving, qualifying and following up on inquiries. A blueprint could show the intended workflow, decision points, information requirements and where a suitable system might help. A development plan could prioritize process agreement, training needs and later tool decisions.

The example illustrates how the outputs could support a decision. It does not show that the process was launched, staff were trained or inquiries increased.

(08)

What we may need from you

Share the business priority or project plan, the work currently involved, existing process notes and the people responsible for relevant decisions. Where useful, we may also need descriptions of current tools and how information is shared. We discuss required access and privacy arrangements before you provide internal records.

(09)

What we agree before starting

The priority and boundaries

Define which capability or workflow is being reviewed and which teams, processes or systems are in scope.

Inputs and participation

Agree the available records, interviews or working sessions, key reviewers and information-handling arrangements.

Outputs and review points

Specify the blueprint, development plan, delivery format, responsibilities, price and expected schedule.

What happens after planning

Identify any implementation, tool configuration, staff training, documentation or follow-up as separate tasks unless expressly included.

(10)

Common questions

Do we need a finished growth roadmap first?

No. You can start with an agreed priority, existing plan or specific operating problem. We discuss whether there is enough clarity to define the capabilities needed. If the strategic direction itself is unsettled, roadmap planning may be a separate starting point.

How is this different from growth strategy?

Growth strategy decides the priorities and sequence of business initiatives. Capability development examines the people, processes, technology and oversight needed to carry out those choices. They are separate services with different deliverables.

Does this service include installing software or changing our processes?

Only if agreed. A blueprint or plan does not include implementation by default. Any setup, redesign, training or support must have a defined scope and owner.

Can you work with our existing tools and team?

Yes. We review existing workflows and tools against the need, with access and stakeholder input agreed first. New technology is not always necessary.

How do you decide which gaps matter most?

We compare gaps against the business priority, risks, dependencies and effort, and explain the evidence and open decisions behind the proposed order.

How are price and timing decided?

They depend on the systems and processes in scope, available information and deliverables. We agree terms and review points before work starts.
