SVC-17DCustomer Value, Retention & Asset Production

Build stronger customer relationships after the sale.

Keeping in touch with customers takes more than sending occasional reminders. People need useful support, clear communication and confidence that your business still understands their needs after the first purchase.

BGI Solutions helps you review how existing customers are supported, where relationships may need attention and when an additional service could genuinely help. We can also plan a responsible way to invite feedback, referrals or customer stories.

Talk to UsExplore All Services

(01)

When this service may help

Customers stop engaging

You may not know when interest fades, whether support needs changed or why an expected follow-up did not happen.

Follow-up depends on memory

Different team members contact customers at different times, or no one clearly owns the next conversation.

Additional needs are easy to miss

Customers may need more help, but there is no clear way to check whether an offer is suitable before suggesting it.

Happy customers have no simple way to share feedback

Your team needs an appropriate process for genuine reviews, referrals or stories, without pressure or invented praise.

(02)

Start with one customer relationship problem

Tell us which customer group, product or service needs attention. We can discuss a focused review of existing relationships, available records and the follow-up decisions that matter.

This service plans customer retention, suitable expansion and advocacy practices. It does not automatically include contacting your customers, operating a support desk, sending campaigns or guaranteeing repeat purchases.

(03)

How a customer relationship review can work

We begin by understanding what customers need after the initial purchase. These four stages describe a possible approach; the agreed scope defines the records, conversations and decisions included.

  1. 01

    Understand the customer relationship

    Review customer groups, service expectations, available support history and relevant purchasing or usage records. Identify what is known about continued value and where important information is missing.

  2. 02

    Map follow-up and support needs

    Look at useful contact points, unanswered questions, complaints, handoffs and moments when a customer may need help. Separate observed concerns from assumptions that require further checking.

  3. 03

    Consider appropriate next steps

    Identify possible improvements in follow-up and support. Where evidence suggests a further customer need, outline a suitable way to discuss an additional service. Plan genuine feedback or referral requests only where appropriate.

  4. 04

    Agree ownership and review measures

    Define who approves messages, handles customer responses, follows up on problems and reviews the work. Choose realistic measures based on available records, with a clear review period and responsibilities.

(04)

Focus on value, not just more messages

Useful retention work starts with the reason customers continue to choose a business. Contact frequency alone does not show customer value. Review measures may include continued use, repeat business or feedback, but only where definitions and records are reliable.

(05)

What you can receive

Three proposed outputs help turn scattered follow-up into a clearer plan, identify legitimate further needs and set respectful rules for customer advocacy.

01

Retention and Follow-up Plan

A plan for useful customer contact: when support or information may be needed, what to communicate, who owns the next action and which warning signs deserve attention.

The plan can distinguish routine follow-up from issues requiring a response or escalation. It identifies the agreed measures and the records needed to review whether the process is working.

02

Expansion Opportunity Review

A review of additional customer needs that the business may be able to serve. It distinguishes evidence of a suitable fit from commercial assumptions, and identifies questions to confirm before making an offer.

The review is not a forecast of sales. Any new offer, pricing change or customer outreach is subject to business approval and an agreed implementation scope.

03

Advocacy Workflow

A proposed process for inviting genuine feedback, appropriate referrals or permissioned customer stories. It can define who asks, what permissions are recorded, how responses are handled and when an issue must be escalated.

Customer quotes, names, photos, logos and stories must not be published without appropriate evidence and permissions. Operating the workflow over time is separate from designing it unless included in the agreement.

(06)

A sample project

Sample project

A service business hears from customers only when something goes wrong

Imagine a business that delivers a recurring service. Customers receive an initial welcome, but later contact is inconsistent. Staff may learn about a problem only when a customer complains or decides not to continue.

A focused review might map the moments when customers need an update, assign responsibility for follow-up and identify which service questions should prompt a conversation. The business might also consider when it is appropriate to request genuine feedback.

The retention plan would document the proposed contact steps; the expansion review would assess suitable further needs; and the advocacy workflow would clarify permissions. Any changes would still require approval, implementation and review.

(07)

What we may need from you

Share the customer groups you want to understand, the services they use, existing follow-up materials, common questions and approved customer-feedback processes. Relevant records must be shared and handled appropriately.

(08)

What we agree before starting

Customer focus

Define the customer group, relationship stage, support concerns and outcomes worth reviewing.

Information and permissions

Confirm available records, lawful access, confidentiality, consent needs and gaps in the evidence.

Outputs and owners

Agree the retention plan, expansion review and advocacy workflow; identify approvers and people responsible for any subsequent customer contact.

Measures and terms

Define review measures, timing, any content or system changes, production scope, revisions, handover, price and ongoing support boundaries.

(09)

Questions about customer retention and advocacy

What does customer retention mean?

It means customers continue a suitable relationship with your business over time. The useful measure depends on your business model and reliable records.

How do you identify opportunities to help existing customers further?

We can review service use, customer needs and available feedback. A possible additional service should address a real need; fit and commercial assumptions must be checked.

Does this service include sending follow-up messages?

Not automatically. We can plan timing, content and responsibility. Writing, sending or operating communications is included only when agreed.

Can you collect reviews, referrals or customer stories?

An advocacy workflow can define appropriate requests and review steps. Any collection, publication or use of personal information needs the relevant approvals and permissions.

Do we need a customer management system?

Not necessarily. We can start with the records and practices you already have. Data integration, software setup and ongoing administration are separate scope decisions.

Can you guarantee more repeat purchases or referrals?

No. Customer decisions depend on delivered value, service quality, fit and many other factors. We agree how any implemented changes will be reviewed.
