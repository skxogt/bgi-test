SVC-19EAI, Optimization & Continuous Intelligence

Find a practical starting point for AI.

You may have an idea for using AI, but the first question is whether it can help with a real task in your business. A useful decision starts with the work, the information it needs and the results you can check.

BGI Solutions helps you assess a defined AI opportunity before committing to a tool or rollout. We look at how the work happens today, what may be suitable for AI and which privacy, accuracy and human-review safeguards are needed.

(01)

When an AI readiness review may help

You have a repetitive task

Your team spends time on similar steps, but you do not yet know which parts are suitable for AI assistance.

You have an AI tool in mind

You want to check whether it fits your information, workflow, permissions and real business need.

Your information is not ready

Important records may be incomplete, sensitive or scattered across systems, making a trial hard to evaluate.

You need a responsible test

You want a clear owner, human checks and criteria for deciding whether to stop, revise or expand an AI pilot.

(02)

Start with one specific task

Tell us which task you want to improve, who does it now and what a useful result would look like. We can discuss a focused assessment without committing to a wider AI program.

The agreed scope confirms the tasks and sources reviewed, the outputs to be prepared and any limited pilot support. Building, integrating or running an AI system is separate unless expressly included.

(03)

How an AI readiness assessment works

We begin with a practical decision: is this AI idea worth testing, what must be checked first and who remains responsible for the outcome? The agreed scope can follow four stages.

  1. 01

    Define the job to be done

    Map the current task, people, inputs, decisions and problems. Identify candidate AI uses and agree what improvement would matter, without assuming automation is the best answer.

  2. 02

    Review information and access

    Check which documents, data and systems the idea would rely on. Identify gaps, quality issues, permissions, sensitive information and tools that may be unsuitable for a test.

  3. 03

    Assess risks and human oversight

    Consider likely errors, privacy and security needs, reliability, review effort and the consequences of a wrong answer. Specify which decisions need a person to verify, approve, correct or stop an output.

  4. 04

    Plan an appropriate pilot

    If testing is justified, define a narrow use, sample tasks, success and failure criteria, responsible reviewers, evidence to capture and stopping rules. Record what would need further approval before wider use.

(04)

A readiness review is a decision aid

A positive finding does not prove a tool will be reliable in every situation. The output can support a pilot decision, identify work to complete first or recommend not proceeding with AI for that task.

(05)

What the assessment can produce

These are possible outputs, not an automatic four-part package. Your proposal identifies which reviews, checklists and plans are included and who will confirm the findings.

01

Workflow and Use-Case Review

A clear record of the existing task, where time or confusion occurs and which AI-assisted uses may be worth considering. The review distinguishes the actual work from assumptions about what technology could do.

Possible next steps are compared by relevance, available evidence, operational fit and feasibility. A non-AI improvement may be the better recommendation.

02

Information and Access Assessment

An outline of the information, systems and permissions needed for a proposed use. It records missing, unreliable or sensitive inputs that could make testing unhelpful or inappropriate.

The assessment identifies issues to resolve before test access, including data handling, tool suitability and who may approve use of particular records.

03

Risk and Human-Oversight Checklist

A practical list of potential errors, privacy and security issues, review duties and decisions that must stay with a person. It records what needs checking and when a test must be paused or escalated.

04

Pilot Plan and Evaluation Criteria

A limited test proposal defining tasks, reviewers, approved information, measures of usefulness, failure conditions and the evidence needed to decide whether to stop, change or expand the trial.

(06)

Decide what a small AI pilot should prove

A pilot should answer a specific question with checkable evidence, rather than simply show that a tool can produce output.

What an evaluation plan should cover

Set criteria suited to the task, such as output accuracy, required human corrections, handling of exceptions, time spent reviewing and any safety or privacy concerns. The business agrees the review period and acceptable performance before testing.

What belongs in the project agreement

Confirm the use case, assessment outputs, available information, access permissions, reviewer roles, approvals, schedule, price and any pilot support. Software purchases, integration, development, deployment, model training and live operation are included only when expressly agreed.

Keep people responsible for decisions

AI can generate incomplete or incorrect content. A person should review outputs where errors matter, and sensitive data must not be shared with tools without an approved basis and controls. The required safeguards depend on the intended use.

(07)

A sample project

Sample project

A team wants help drafting routine customer replies

Imagine a service business that often answers similar questions. A review could map the inquiry types, approved answers, exceptions and the staff member responsible for final communication. Some questions may require a human response from the start.

A possible pilot plan could test draft replies against approved examples, track corrections and escalation needs, and define when the test stops. Whether the tool is built or deployed would be a separate decision.

It is not a BGI Solutions client case, completed deployment, measured outcome or promise of AI performance.

(08)

Questions about AI readiness

Do we need to have an AI tool selected?

No. Start with the task and the result you want. The review can consider whether AI is suitable before you choose any tool.

Can we start if our information is incomplete?

Yes, we can discuss the task. The assessment identifies which gaps matter and whether they must be resolved before a useful or responsible pilot.

Does an assessment include building an AI system?

Not automatically. The agreed scope says whether you receive an assessment, a pilot plan or any pilot support. Development, integration, deployment and ongoing operation need their own agreed terms.

Can you guarantee that AI will save time or reduce costs?

No. Results depend on the task, information quality, safeguards and review effort. A pilot can measure relevant outcomes, but an assessment is not proof of financial or operational gains.

What if AI makes a mistake?

The review considers error consequences, human oversight, escalation and stopping rules. The pilot plan identifies how outputs would be checked before any broader adoption decision.

Will you need access to private business or customer data?

Not necessarily at the first conversation. We first identify what evidence is needed. Any sensitive data use, sharing or tool access must follow approved permissions and security arrangements.
