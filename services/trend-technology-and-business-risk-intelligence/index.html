SVC-08BMarket, Customer & Competitive Intelligence

Know which changes deserve your attention.

A new technology, changing customer behavior or a business risk can raise an important question: should you respond now, watch the situation or learn more first? BGI Solutions helps you examine relevant changes and understand their possible effect on your decisions.

We review available information, separate well-supported signals from speculation and explain what may matter to your business. The agreed findings help you decide what to investigate, prepare for or monitor next.

(01)

When this research can help

A technology may change how work gets done

You want to understand practical uses, limits and risks before making an investment.

Customer needs or buying habits appear to be shifting

You need to understand the strength of the evidence and what the shift could mean for your offer.

New external risks concern your team

You want to assess how a regulatory, supplier, economic or market change could affect your plans.

Different reports point in different directions

You need a clear view of source dates, assumptions and which signals deserve further attention.

(02)

Start with the business decision

Tell us what has changed or what you are worried about. We can discuss the business decision, the relevant trends or risks, the period to examine and the sources that may help answer the question.

This service supports research and interpretation. It does not automatically include legal or financial advice, buying new technology, making operational changes or continuous monitoring. Those activities need a clearly agreed scope and, where appropriate, specialist review.

(03)

How the research takes shape

A useful review begins with a defined question, not a long list of headlines. These four stages describe a possible approach. Your proposal confirms the topics, research period, information sources and review depth.

  1. 01

    Define the question and what matters

    Clarify which decision the research should support and which trends, technologies or risks could affect it. Agree the time period, relevant market, business context and criteria for deciding whether a change matters.

  2. 02

    Review signals and their sources

    Examine relevant information from agreed sources. Record the publication or collection date, the period covered and any limits. Distinguish an observed change from a forecast, an opinion or a claim that cannot yet be confirmed.

  3. 03

    Explore implications and possible scenarios

    Consider how the change might affect customers, costs, delivery, competition or other factors relevant to the question. Describe plausible conditions and dependencies without treating a possible future as certain.

  4. 04

    Explain priorities and review triggers

    Organize the findings, identify which signals need attention and define what new information could change the view. Explain the decisions raised, what still needs checking and possible triggers for another review.

(04)

What you can use the findings for

The findings can help you decide whether to investigate an option, prepare a response, ask for specialist advice or revisit a decision later. Where information is weak or changing quickly, we explain the uncertainty instead of presenting a confident prediction.

(05)

What you can receive

Three proposed outputs organize the evidence, possible implications and next checks. Your proposal confirms which are included, how detailed they will be and whether any follow-up research is part of the work.

Trend and Risk Matrix

A structured view of the external changes relevant to your question. Each entry can identify the signal, its source and date, why it may matter, the likely business exposure and the strength or limits of available evidence.

The matrix helps you compare signals without assuming that every headline is equally important. It also records where a source is outdated, too broad for your business or not sufficient to support a conclusion.

Scenarios

Descriptions of possible conditions and what they could mean for your business. Each scenario explains its assumptions, relevant dependencies, possible implications and the information that would support or challenge it.

Scenarios are tools for thinking through uncertainty. They are not forecasts, guarantees or claims that a particular event will happen.

(06)

Monitoring Priorities

A focused list of developments to watch, why they matter and which changes may trigger review or action. It can identify suggested indicators, useful source types, proposed review points and the person or team responsible for decisions.

These priorities describe a monitoring plan. They do not mean BGI Solutions is continuously tracking events or sending alerts unless that work is separately agreed.

How we keep the review useful

We identify source dates and coverage, distinguish observations from interpretation and label assumptions. Important gaps stay visible. The work supports the decision in the agreed brief rather than claiming to predict the future or solve every outside risk.

(07)

A sample project

Sample project

A service business is considering a new scheduling tool

Imagine a business hearing that new scheduling technology may change customer expectations. The owner wants to know whether to invest now, improve the current process or wait for better information.

A project could review dated reports about relevant tools and customer behavior, compare their limitations and examine internal constraints identified by the business. Scenarios could consider several possible adoption conditions and what each would require.

The Trend and Risk Matrix could document supported signals and open questions. Monitoring Priorities might identify what to check again before making a larger commitment. No actual market data, implementation or customer results are claimed in this illustration.

(08)

What we may need from you

Share the decision you face, your business goals, the changes you have noticed and any reports or information already available. We discuss which records, systems or source permissions are needed before you provide access to sensitive material.

(09)

What we agree before starting

Question and scope

Identify the decision, relevant trends, technologies or risks, affected business activities and research period.

Evidence and methods

Agree the source types, available records, evaluation criteria, required access and how uncertainty will be recorded.

Outputs and review

Confirm which matrix, scenarios and monitoring priorities will be delivered, in what format, and when findings are discussed.

Commercial and follow-on terms

Agree the price, expected schedule, responsibilities and whether further research, monitoring, pilots or implementation will be separate work.

(10)

Common questions

Can you tell us which trends will happen next?

We can review available evidence and develop scenarios, but we cannot predict the future with certainty. Findings explain what is supported, what is assumed and which new signals would change the assessment.

How do you decide which risks deserve attention?

We begin with your business decision and agree criteria such as relevance, possible impact, exposure and the strength of the evidence. A signal may deserve further investigation even when its likelihood is uncertain.

What if sources disagree or information changes quickly?

We compare source dates, coverage, methods and important limitations. Differences are explained rather than hidden. Where confidence is low, recommendations remain provisional and may include a further research step.

Will you continuously monitor our industry or send alerts?

Not unless it is included in a separate agreed scope. This service can define monitoring priorities and review triggers. Ongoing tracking, reports and alerts require clear responsibilities, sources, schedule and terms.

Does this include an AI pilot or technology implementation?

The research examines relevant technologies and risks. An AI readiness assessment, pilot, software purchase or implementation is a separate decision and must be included explicitly in the project agreement.

How are pricing and timing decided?

They depend on the question, number of topics, available evidence, research period, selected outputs and review arrangements. We agree the scope, price and expected schedule before work begins.
