SVC-02ADiagnose, Prioritize & Strategize

Decide which growth opportunities deserve attention first.

You may see several ways to grow your business, but not every idea deserves the same investment. BGI Solutions helps you identify possible opportunities, compare what supports them and see which options are worth investigating or pursuing.

We look at potential value, available evidence, feasibility, risk and dependencies. The goal is a clearer choice about where to focus, with the assumptions and unanswered questions visible.

Talk to UsExplore All Services

(01)

When this service can help

You have more ideas than resources

You are considering several offers, customer groups, channels or improvements, but cannot take on everything at once.

You want to find new possibilities

You know growth matters but need to identify plausible opportunities before comparing them.

People favor different options

Your team needs common criteria and a clear explanation of the evidence behind each choice.

You are approaching a major commitment

You want to examine risks, practical requirements and gaps in the information before investing.

(02)

The decision this work supports

Which opportunities deserve attention first, what makes them promising, and what should be checked before committing? An opportunity can look attractive yet depend on an untested assumption or a capability the business does not have. We make those differences easier to see.

(03)

How opportunity discovery and prioritization works

The work starts with your business goal and the decision you need to make. The sources, depth and methods depend on what is already known and what the agreed scope needs to establish.

  1. 01

    Define the goal and opportunities

    Agree what growth means for this decision and which options are relevant. We can work with ideas already under consideration or examine possible options within an agreed research boundary. We identify the intended customers, business activities and timeframe being considered.

  2. 02

    Review value and supporting evidence

    Compare how each opportunity could help the intended customer or business. Review existing records, research or other relevant sources, distinguishing observed information from estimates and assumptions.

  3. 03

    Examine feasibility, risks and dependencies

    Consider the resources, skills, processes, technology and external conditions each option may require. Record important risks and dependencies so that an attractive idea does not appear ready when essential conditions remain uncertain.

  4. 04

    Compare priorities and next checks

    Apply agreed criteria consistently and explain why some opportunities appear more promising or practical than others. Identify the assumptions that could change the order and what needs validation before a firm commitment.

(04)

A comparison you can explain

We do not treat a score as proof. A priority judgment should show the criteria used, the information behind it and the limits of that information. If two options are too uncertain to compare fairly, the work can recommend what to investigate rather than forcing a ranking.

(05)

What you can receive

Two central outputs make the comparison useful. Your proposal confirms which deliverables are included, the options covered and the level of detail. Supporting notes may be part of those outputs rather than separate products.

Opportunity Map

An organized view of the possible growth opportunities. It describes each option, who it could serve, what potential value it offers and which evidence or assumptions support it. Important risks, dependencies and questions can be shown alongside the options.

Use it to see the range of choices without losing track of why each one is being considered.

Priority Matrix

A side-by-side comparison using criteria agreed for your decision. These may include potential value, strength of evidence, feasibility, risks and dependencies. The matrix shows how the options compare and where missing information affects confidence.

Use it to understand which options may deserve attention, which need validation and what could change the priorities.

(06)

What makes a useful comparison

Potential value

What meaningful customer or business need the opportunity could address, and why it matters.

Evidence and uncertainty

What is known, when it was learned and what remains an assumption.

Feasibility and dependencies

Which resources, capabilities, approvals or other conditions would be needed.

Risk and decision readiness

What might prevent success and which checks are required before a commitment.

(07)

What these outputs do not promise

The map and matrix help organize a decision. They do not guarantee revenue, prove that an opportunity will succeed or replace the research, testing or planning that a high-stakes decision may require.

(08)

Illustrative opportunity comparison

This example illustrates how the service might be scoped. It is not a client engagement or evidence of achieved results.

A business has three possible next projects

A service business is considering a new offer, a clearer website and a different marketing channel. It wants to know which deserves attention first. The opportunity map could describe the purpose and expected demands of each option.

The priority matrix could then compare customer relevance, available evidence, cost or effort assumptions, operational readiness and risks. If the new offer lacks customer evidence or the channel depends on an untested message, those uncertainties would be shown rather than hidden behind a score.

The result would support a decision about which option to investigate first. It would not be a claim that any option has already produced growth.

(09)

What we may need from you

Share your goals, existing ideas, intended customers, relevant business or market information and any previous research. We may also need to understand current resources, constraints and decisions already made. We discuss which records or access are necessary and how information will be shared before you provide it.

(10)

What we agree before starting

Decision and coverage

Define the goal, opportunities to discover or compare, the period considered and any options excluded.

Criteria and evidence

Agree what matters in the comparison, which sources will be reviewed and how major unknowns will be recorded.

Outputs and responsibilities

Confirm the selected deliverables, review points, inputs, price and expected schedule.

Next-step boundary

Prioritization informs a choice. A detailed strategy, new market research, testing, production or implementation is a separate agreed scope when required.

(11)

Common questions

Do we need to have a list of opportunities already?

No. We can discuss identifying plausible opportunities within a defined scope. If you already have options, the work can focus on comparing them.

How is this different from a growth assessment?

A business growth assessment investigates what may be causing a problem. Opportunity discovery and prioritization compares possible ways forward. Findings from an assessment can inform this work, but an assessment is not always required first.

What if we do not have enough information to compare options?

We record important gaps and make uncertainty visible. The work may identify which questions to research or test before deciding, rather than recommend a confident ranking the evidence cannot support.

Will you tell us which opportunity to pursue?

The agreed analysis can explain priorities and the reasons behind them. Your business makes the final decision, taking into account your circumstances, resources and appetite for risk.

Does this include a complete strategy or implementation?

Not automatically. A growth roadmap sets the plan for accepted priorities. Building a website, running a campaign or making operational changes requires its own agreed scope.

How are pricing and timing decided?

They depend on the number and complexity of options, research required, available information and review arrangements. We agree the defined work, price and expected schedule before starting.
