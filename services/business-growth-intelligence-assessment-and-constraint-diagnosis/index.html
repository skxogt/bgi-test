SVC-01ADiagnose, Prioritize & Strategize

Find what is slowing your business growth.

Before investing in another change, understand what may be holding growth back. BGI Solutions reviews the available business information, examines possible causes and explains which issues deserve attention first.

You do not need to know the answer before you contact us. Tell us what is happening, what you have tried and what you need to understand. We can discuss whether an assessment is a useful starting point.

Talk to UsExplore All Services

(01)

When this assessment can help

An assessment can be useful when the problem is important but the explanation is not yet clear.

Growth has slowed

Sales, inquiries or another important business measure are below expectations, but the reasons are uncertain.

People disagree about the cause

Different teams have different explanations, and you need to compare them with the available evidence.

Previous changes did not solve the problem

You have tried several approaches and want to know which assumptions still need investigation.

A larger decision is coming

You want to understand the problem before committing more time, money or resources to another solution.

(02)

What the assessment is designed to answer

What appears to be wrong? What evidence supports that explanation? What needs attention first? The work separates visible symptoms from possible underlying causes and records important questions the evidence cannot yet answer.

The goal is a clearer basis for your next decision, not a promise of a particular business result.

(03)

How the assessment works

The investigation follows four connected steps. The depth of each step depends on the question, the agreed scope and the information available.

  1. 01

    Define the question

    Clarify the business goal, the concern you want investigated and the parts of the business included in the assessment. Agree what decision the findings should help you make and which topics are outside the scope.

  2. 02

    Review the available information

    Examine relevant records, previous findings and explanations from the people involved. Record what was reviewed, the period it covers, what it appears to show and where the information is incomplete.

  3. 03

    Examine possible causes

    Compare plausible explanations and the ways they may connect. Distinguish symptoms from possible causes, look for competing explanations and note which findings are well supported and which require further investigation.

  4. 04

    Explain the findings

    Bring the findings together in a clear, prioritized account. Review what the assessment supports, its limitations and the decisions it raises. Discuss which questions might need more data, research or a separately scoped next project.

(04)

Evidence matters as much as the conclusion

A persuasive explanation is not always a proven explanation. Findings should show the information behind them, the assumptions made and important gaps. Where the data is limited or conflicting, conclusions remain preliminary.

The assessment can identify where attention may be worthwhile; it does not, by itself, validate every proposed solution or establish that a future action will succeed.

(05)

What you can receive

The assessment may produce four connected outputs. Each has a different purpose. Your proposal confirms which deliverables are included and how much detail each will contain.

01

Business Growth Intelligence Assessment

A structured review of the business situation, the goal and the relevant information. It records the question investigated, what was examined and significant gaps. This creates a shared starting point for understanding the problem.

02

Growth Constraint Map

A visual map of the main obstacles and how they may relate to one another. It helps distinguish what the business is experiencing from the factors that may be causing it. Connections remain hypotheses where the evidence is not yet strong enough to confirm them.

03

Root-Cause Analysis

An explanation of the most plausible causes of the priority issue. It compares supporting evidence with alternative explanations and identifies unanswered questions, so you can see how much confidence to place in each finding.

04

Priority Findings Report

A focused account of what appears to deserve attention first and why. It connects the main findings to the decisions you face, highlights relevant uncertainties and identifies any further information needed before action.

How the deliverables fit together

The assessment records the situation and evidence. The constraint map shows possible relationships. The root-cause analysis examines likely explanations. The priority findings report helps decide which issue deserves closer attention next. They are complementary outputs, not four claims that the cause has been conclusively proven.

What we may need from you

We may ask about your goals, products or services, intended customers, sales or marketing records, and what you have already tried. Depending on the question, input from people familiar with the process may also help. We discuss what is necessary and how to share information before you provide access to business or customer records.

(06)

An illustrative assessment question

This example explains a possible scope. It is not an actual client case study and does not report achieved results.

“We receive inquiries, but too few become customers. What should we examine?”

An assessment could review where inquiries come from, whether they match the intended customer, how clearly the offer is explained and what happens after someone contacts the business. The work would compare possible reasons for the low conversion from inquiry to customer and explain which explanations the available records support.

The findings could identify a follow-up problem, a mismatch in customer fit, unclear offer information or a combination of factors. They would also record questions that cannot be answered from the available information.

(07)

What we agree before starting

The decision and boundaries

Confirm the problem to investigate, the business activities in scope and what the findings need to inform.

Information and responsibilities

Agree which records are relevant, what access is required, who provides the information and how it will be handled.

Deliverables and review points

Confirm the selected outputs, their level of detail, review stages, price and expected schedule.

The next step

Assessment and diagnosis are this service’s focus. A full growth strategy, detailed roadmap, additional research or implementation must be separately agreed if needed.

(08)

What happens after the assessment?

You can use the findings to make a decision, gather missing information or discuss another defined service. There is no requirement to purchase a further service. Any additional work is proposed and agreed separately.

(09)

Common questions

Do we need complete data to begin?

Not necessarily. We discuss what is available and whether it can support a useful assessment. Missing information is recorded, and conclusions remain preliminary where the evidence is limited.

Does this include a complete growth strategy?

No. The assessment investigates the current situation and likely causes. A detailed growth strategy and roadmap turn accepted findings into choices and a plan, and are covered by a separate scope.

Will the assessment tell us exactly what to change?

It can clarify where the problem may lie and which issues deserve attention. Some findings support a next decision; others call for further research or testing before an intervention can be justified.

Can you work with assessments or reports we already have?

Tell us what you have, when it was prepared and what question it addressed. We can discuss reviewing its relevance and limitations. Any new research or validation needed is identified in the scope.

Will we have to buy another service afterward?

No. You choose what happens next. We explain any related service that could help with a separate decision, but additional work is discussed and agreed before it begins.

How are price and timing decided?

They depend on the question, information to review, selected deliverables and review arrangements. We discuss the scope, price and expected schedule before work starts.
