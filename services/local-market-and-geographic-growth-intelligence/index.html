SVC-07BMarket, Customer & Competitive Intelligence

Compare markets before deciding where to grow.

Where should your business focus next? Which areas offer a reason to investigate further, and what would it take to serve them well? BGI Solutions helps you compare geographic opportunities using the evidence and practical requirements relevant to your decision.

We can review selected locations, demand indicators, competing offers and the requirements of delivering your service there. The findings help you understand the trade-offs, important gaps and next checks before committing resources.

When geographic research can help

You are comparing possible service areas

You want to evaluate where demand and competitive conditions may justify closer attention.

You are considering entering another location

You need to understand practical delivery requirements and key uncertainties before deciding.

Your current market focus feels too broad

You want a clearer basis for choosing which areas deserve research or action first.

Several locations look promising

You need a consistent comparison rather than relying on isolated statistics or assumptions.

Start with the location decision

Share the areas you are considering and what the decision means for your business. We can discuss the customers, services, comparison criteria and available information that belong in the research.

This service supports geographic choices. It does not, by itself, establish demand, authorize market entry, create local listings or implement an expansion plan. Any subsequent marketing, operational setup or launch requires a separately agreed scope.

How geographic research takes shape

The question comes first: whether to focus, compare or explore expansion. These four stages describe a possible approach. We agree the markets, criteria, sources and depth of analysis before work begins.

  1. 01

    Define the decision and comparison areas

    Clarify which locations to examine, the offer or customer group in question and the choice the research needs to support. Agree criteria such as customer demand, competition, delivery requirements and risks. We also define the geographic boundaries and research period.

  2. 02

    Review demand and competitive conditions

    Review available demand indicators, relevant customer characteristics and competing alternatives. Compare like with like where possible and note differences in source dates, geographic coverage and measurement methods. A signal of interest is not proof of actual sales.

  3. 03

    Examine delivery feasibility and constraints

    Consider the practical requirements of serving each area, such as staffing or partner capacity, travel or fulfillment, local operating rules where relevant and the costs or dependencies the client identifies. Specialized legal or regulatory review is separate work unless explicitly included.

  4. 04

    Compare areas and explain the next checks

    Organize the evidence against the agreed criteria. Identify areas that may deserve priority, those needing more information and assumptions that could change the conclusion. Review the findings with you before a decision about market entry or implementation.

What the comparison can support

You gain a structured view of where a geographic option appears promising, where it may be difficult to serve and what should be validated before acting. A recommendation stays conditional when sources are incomplete, old or not comparable.

What you can receive

Two proposed outputs help turn location data into a decision you can review. Your proposal confirms which outputs are included, which areas they cover and how the findings are supported.

Geographic Comparisons

A side-by-side assessment of selected locations using criteria agreed for your business. It can examine available demand indicators, competition, audience fit and the practical requirements of serving each area.

The comparison identifies the sources and dates behind each observation, where the evidence is comparable and where it is not. It also shows information gaps or feasibility issues that need checking rather than hiding them behind a single score.

Market Priorities

A reasoned view of which areas deserve further investigation and why. It connects each priority to the comparison criteria, business objectives, evidence confidence and important dependencies.

The priorities are decision support, not a prediction of revenue or a guarantee that expansion will work. They can distinguish areas worth validating now from those to revisit later or rule out based on the available information.

How we make the findings reviewable

Consistent criteria

Explain why each location is included and which measures or conditions matter to your decision.

Traceable evidence

Show source dates, geographic coverage, important assumptions and any limits in the data.

Delivery reality

Consider whether the business could reasonably serve each area and what remains unverified.

The proposed outputs do not automatically include site selection, local advertising, licensing advice, on-site field research or ongoing market monitoring. Those activities, where suitable, need an explicit scope.

Illustrative geographic decision

The following situation is an editorial example of a possible project. It is not completed client research, a verified market comparison or evidence of achieved results.

A service business is comparing two nearby areas

Imagine a business that currently works in one area and is considering expanding service into either Area A or Area B. The business sees potential demand in both, but it does not yet know how competition, travel requirements or operating capacity compare.

A project could review available demand signals and relevant alternatives for both areas, then document the sources and how comparable they are. It could also examine practical assumptions, such as travel time, scheduling and the people or equipment needed to provide the service.

A geographic comparison might reveal that one area is worth investigating first while the other requires further feasibility checks. Market priorities would explain the reasoning and unanswered questions. No real location, demand figure or result is implied by this example.

What we may need from you

Tell us which areas interest you, who you want to serve, what you offer and why the location decision matters now. Share relevant operating constraints, available capacity and previous research. We discuss what information is needed and how access or permissions will be handled before you share nonpublic records.

What we agree before starting

Areas and decision

Identify the geographic boundaries, customers, offer and choice the research should inform.

Criteria and information

Agree comparison criteria, sources, research period, data limitations and any new information collection.

Outputs and responsibilities

Confirm the geographic comparisons, market priorities, review points, evidence notes and delivery formats.

Commercial and follow-on terms

Confirm price, expected schedule and any separately scoped validation, implementation or continuing monitoring.

Common questions

Can you help us choose between two or more locations?

We can discuss a structured comparison of the areas you are considering. The research explains relevant evidence, trade-offs and feasibility questions, so you can make a better-informed decision. It does not guarantee which area will perform best.

Do we need to know the exact locations in advance?

Not necessarily. Tell us the region, customer group and decision you face. We can discuss whether defining possible areas is part of the agreed research scope before comparing them.

What if local market information is limited?

We explain what the available sources can and cannot support. We record gaps, source dates and assumptions. A market priority may remain provisional, or the next step may be collecting additional information.

Does this include opening a location or launching local marketing?

No, not automatically. This service focuses on geographic assessment and priorities. Business setup, local marketing, licensing review, recruitment and implementation require separate agreement and appropriate specialists where needed.

How is this different from other market research?

Market & Customer Intelligence focuses on customer groups and buying decisions. Competitive Intelligence & Differentiation Strategy examines alternatives and how to compete. This service compares geographic choices, including the practical ability to serve them.

How are timing and pricing decided?

They depend on the number of areas, the available evidence, comparison criteria, feasibility work and selected deliverables. We agree the scope, price, expected schedule and review points before work starts.
