SVC-05BMarket, Customer & Competitive Intelligence

Understand your customers and what matters to them.

Who is most likely to buy from you? What problem are they trying to solve? What helps them choose, and what makes them hesitate? BGI Solution helps you examine these questions using the customer and market information relevant to your decision.

We can review what you already know, identify important gaps and organize findings into useful customer profiles and buying-decision maps. The goal is to give you a clearer basis for deciding whom to serve and what those customers need to understand.

Talk to UsExplore All Services

(01)

When customer research can help

You serve several customer groups

You need to decide which groups matter most to the offer or business question in front of you.

Prospects ask the same questions

You want to understand the concerns, comparisons and information that shape their decisions.

Your message may not fit the audience

You need to check whether the needs and buying situations you describe match what customers value.

You are considering a new offer or audience

You want to examine demand signals and important unknowns before investing in changes.

(02)

Start with the decision you need to make

Tell us whether you are choosing a customer segment, reviewing an existing offer or trying to understand why interested people do not move forward. We can discuss which groups, sources and research questions belong in the project.

You do not need to buy a full growth assessment or every market-intelligence service first. This service focuses on customers and their decisions; competitor comparison, geographic research and implementation have their own scopes.

(03)

How the customer research takes shape

The project begins with a business question, not a fixed research method. The steps below describe a possible approach; we agree the activities, sources and depth before work begins.

  1. 01

    Define the customer and market question

    Clarify what you need to decide, which offer or service it concerns and the customers or markets to examine. Agree any useful segments, such as customer need, buying situation or business type, without assuming those groups are meaningful before examining the evidence.

  2. 02

    Review the information available

    Identify relevant existing records, customer feedback, sales questions and other available sources. Agree whether the project needs additional information collection. We examine each source for its date, coverage, relevance and limitations, and identify information that is missing or outdated.

  3. 03

    Examine needs and buying decisions

    Look for patterns in customer needs, buying triggers, expectations, concerns and objections. Distinguish what the information supports from interpretations that need checking. Avoid treating a few comments or incomplete records as proof about an entire market.

  4. 04

    Explain the findings and next questions

    Organize the agreed outputs so you can see the priority customer groups, how a buying decision may unfold and what remains uncertain. Review the findings together and identify which decisions they can support, along with any further research or validation needed.

(04)

What makes the findings useful

The value is not just a list of customer characteristics. Useful research connects a specific customer need to the evidence behind it, the decision you are considering and the limits of what can reasonably be concluded.

(05)

What you can receive

Two connected outputs can help you understand who the audience is and how it chooses. The proposal confirms which outputs are included, what they contain and how much research supports them.

Customer Profiles

Descriptions of customer groups relevant to the agreed business question. A profile can summarize needs, common buying situations, goals, questions and factors that may influence customer fit. Each profile should distinguish evidence-supported observations from working assumptions.

Use these profiles to discuss whom a service is designed for, what each group needs to understand and where the available information is still too limited to draw a firm conclusion.

Buying-Decision Maps

A view of how a customer may move from recognizing a need to comparing options and deciding whether to proceed. A map can highlight questions, concerns, decision criteria, information gaps and points where a customer may hesitate.

Use a decision map to identify what a future website, offer or sales conversation may need to explain. The map describes the decision journey supported by the research; it is not a promise that changing content will increase sales.

(06)

How the evidence is presented

Source and timing

Identify where the information came from, when it was collected or published and what period it covers.

Coverage and confidence

Explain which customers or markets are represented and how limited or uneven evidence affects confidence.

Findings and assumptions

Separate what the research shows from possible explanations, estimates or questions needing further checks.

A brief findings explanation may accompany the two outputs to make their evidence and limitations clear. New interviews, surveys, testing or ongoing research are included only if expressly agreed in the project scope.

(07)

A sample project

Sample project

One service, two very different types of buyer

Imagine a service business whose inquiries come from individual buyers and managers purchasing on behalf of a company. Both groups ask about the same service, but they may have different approval steps, priorities and concerns.

A research project could examine available inquiry notes and customer feedback, then prepare separate profiles that explain the groups and their likely needs. Buying-decision maps could organize the questions each group may ask and where information or approval could slow the decision. The findings would identify which differences are supported and which need further validation.

The business could use the work to decide which customer questions a later website or offer project should address. Creating new pages or testing the message would be agreed separately.

(08)

What we may need from you

Tell us about the offer, intended customers, business decision and any relevant research already completed. Existing sales questions, inquiry summaries or permitted feedback may help. We will discuss which records are necessary and how access or privacy requirements should be handled before you share sensitive customer information.

(09)

What we agree before starting

Research question and audience

Define the decision, offers, customer groups, markets and time period included.

Sources and methods

Agree what existing information will be reviewed and whether additional research is part of the scope.

Outputs and review points

Specify the selected customer profiles, decision maps, evidence notes, responsibilities and delivery formats.

Commercial and next-step terms

Confirm price, schedule and any separate message testing, website production or continuing research.

(10)

Common questions

Do we need to know our customer segments already?

No. Start with the offer and the decision you face. We can examine possible groups and whether the information supports treating them separately. Any proposed segments remain subject to the evidence available.

Can you use information we already have?

Yes. We can review relevant records or previous research within the agreed scope. We identify what those sources cover, when they were prepared and where important gaps remain.

Does this service include interviews or surveys?

Not automatically. Your proposal explains which sources and methods are included. Any new research, recruitment, permissions and collection arrangements must be agreed in advance.

How is this different from competitor or geographic research?

This service studies customer needs and buying decisions. Competitive Intelligence & Differentiation Strategy compares alternatives and positioning. Local Market & Geographic Growth Intelligence compares locations and market feasibility. These are separate decisions and scopes.

Will this research tell us which message will perform best?

It can help you understand customer questions and develop informed ideas. Predicting which message will perform best requires suitable validation or testing, and results also depend on other factors. Testing is separate work unless included in the brief.

How are timing and pricing agreed?

They depend on the research question, sources, selected customer groups, methods and deliverables. We agree the scope, expected schedule, price and review points before work begins.
