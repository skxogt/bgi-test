SVC-03ADiagnose, Prioritize & Strategize

Turn your growth priorities into a practical plan.

When you know what needs attention but are unsure how to move forward, a clear strategy can help you make choices. BGI Solutions helps you connect business goals and accepted findings to a growth roadmap that shows what to do, in what order and what to review.

We work through priorities, trade-offs, resources and dependencies so the plan explains not only the direction, but also the decisions and conditions behind it.

Talk to UsExplore All Services

(01)

When this service can help

You have findings but need a plan

An assessment or research has raised issues or opportunities, but the next actions are not yet clear.

Too many initiatives are competing

You need to choose where to focus and what may need to wait.

The order of work matters

Some activities depend on people, information, approvals or systems being ready first.

Progress needs a clear review process

You want to know which signals to track and when to revisit the plan.

(02)

What the strategy needs to answer

Which priorities best support the business goal? What are you choosing not to pursue now? What must happen before the next step can begin? Who will make decisions, and what information would show whether the direction still makes sense?

The starting point may be research, an existing business plan or decisions you have already made. A separate BGI Solutions assessment is not a required first step.

(03)

How growth strategy and roadmap planning works

We start with the decision your business needs to make and the information available. The work below describes a possible planning approach; the proposal defines the actual steps, depth and review arrangements.

  1. 01

    Confirm goals and starting evidence

    Clarify the outcome the business wants, the period the plan should cover and the findings or assumptions that matter. Review existing assessments, customer research, performance information or plans for relevance. Identify missing evidence before treating a preference as a fact.

  2. 02

    Make choices and set priorities

    Compare the possible directions against agreed goals, constraints and available resources. Identify the priorities to pursue, the initiatives to defer and the trade-offs behind those choices. Record significant risks and decisions that still need approval.

  3. 03

    Sequence work and responsibilities

    Translate each accepted priority into planned activities, owners, dependencies and review points. Identify prerequisites and decisions that affect timing. The sequence should be practical for the people and systems involved, with assumptions called out where estimates are uncertain.

  4. 04

    Define measures and adjustment points

    Agree what progress should mean, which information can be measured reliably and who will review it. A scorecard can connect the priorities to a small set of useful measures. Record when the business should reconsider a choice because circumstances or evidence have changed.

(04)

A plan that can change when the evidence changes

A roadmap is a decision tool, not a promise that circumstances will stay the same. It should make the next actions clear while allowing the business to review results, risks and new information before committing to later stages.

(05)

What you can receive

Two connected outputs help turn strategic choices into a plan that can be reviewed. Your proposal identifies which items are included, their level of detail and any supporting analysis needed to prepare them.

Growth Roadmap

A sequenced plan showing the priorities the business has chosen and the work proposed to advance them. It can set out phases or milestones, responsible parties, resource assumptions, dependencies and decision points. The roadmap explains what comes first, why the order matters and which decisions must be made before the next stage.

Use it to align the people involved around agreed priorities and the next practical steps, without confusing a proposed task with a completed one.

Growth Scorecard

A review tool that connects each priority to meaningful measures and a review rhythm. It can define the measure, its available baseline, source, owner and review frequency, plus a question or trigger that would prompt another decision. Where reliable starting data is missing, the scorecard identifies what must be established before progress can be judged.

Use it to discuss whether planned actions are progressing and whether the available information supports continuing, changing or stopping an approach.

(06)

What good planning makes visible

Choices

The priorities selected and the trade-offs accepted, not only a long list of ideas.

Sequence

What depends on what, and which steps are not yet ready to begin.

Responsibility

Who would own each decision, activity and review under the agreed arrangements.

Evidence

Which findings support the plan and which important assumptions still need testing.

Review

How new information could change the direction before more resources are committed.

(07)

The roadmap is not implementation

This service develops strategic choices and the proposed plan. Building systems, producing materials, managing campaigns or operating the planned work are included only when separately defined in the project agreement.

(08)

Illustrative planning situation

This example describes a possible project scope. It is not a client case study or a report of achieved growth.

A business has research and several priorities, but no clear sequence

A service business has identified a need to improve how it explains its services, handles inquiries and follows up with prospective customers. Its leaders agree that the issues matter, but they do not know which change should come first.

A strategy project could compare the intended benefits and practical constraints, then choose a limited set of priorities. The growth roadmap could set out the dependencies, proposed owners and review stages. The scorecard could identify suitable measures, such as inquiries reaching a defined next step, while recording any data gaps or follow-up factors that affect interpretation.

The plan would help the business decide what to do next. The illustration does not show that a change was implemented or that any result was achieved.

(09)

What we may need from you

Tell us the business goal, what has been learned so far, which options are under consideration and who needs to approve decisions. Existing research, plans, relevant records and information about resources or constraints can help. We first agree which materials are necessary and how access will be handled.

(10)

What we agree before starting

Planning decision and coverage

Confirm the goals, planning period, business activities and strategic questions included.

Evidence and assumptions

Agree which sources will be used, what needs validation and how uncertainties are recorded.

Outputs and reviews

Specify the roadmap, scorecard, working sessions, review responsibilities, delivery formats, price and expected schedule.

Implementation boundaries

State who will carry out the plan, which later services might help and whether any follow-up is in scope. Further work is not automatic.

(11)

How the work is reviewed

Review the strategic choices and draft sequence at agreed points. Questions about feasibility, ownership or missing information should be resolved or recorded before the final plan is handed over. The business remains responsible for accepting the choices it will pursue.

(12)

Common questions

Do we need a growth assessment first?

No. If you already have useful findings, priorities or research, we can discuss using them as a starting point. If the cause of a business problem is unclear, a separate assessment may be appropriate.

How is this different from opportunity prioritization?

Opportunity prioritization identifies or compares possible directions. Growth strategy uses accepted findings and priorities to make choices and plan the sequence of work. The two services can connect, but they are not a required package.

Does a roadmap include carrying out the work?

Not by default. This service explains the strategy and proposed sequence. The agreement must state any implementation, production, technology setup or ongoing management included.

What if we cannot measure progress yet?

The scorecard can identify measures worth tracking, the records needed and who should establish them. Where the available data cannot support a conclusion, we identify that limitation rather than invent a baseline.

Can the plan change after it is agreed?

Yes. The roadmap can set review points and conditions that would justify a change. Ongoing monitoring, updates or implementation support are separate work unless included in the agreement.

How are price and timing agreed?

They depend on the decision, available information, scope of analysis, deliverables and review arrangements. We agree the price and expected schedule before work begins.
