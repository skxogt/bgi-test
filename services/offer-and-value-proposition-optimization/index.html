SVC-15CVisibility, Trust, Demand & Acquisition

Make your offer easier to understand and choose.

When customers cannot tell what your offer includes or why it suits them, even genuine interest can fade. BGI Solutions helps you review the promise, structure and explanation of an offer so people can make a more informed decision.

We look at the intended customer, the problem being solved, the benefits you can support and the questions buyers still have. Then we develop clearer offer options and a way to check which ideas are worth using. Any implementation or testing is agreed separately in the project scope.

Talk to UsExplore All Services

(01)

When this service may help

People do not understand what they would receive

Your offer describes activities or features, but the scope, outcomes and next steps are difficult to picture.

Different buyers need different things

A single package or message may be too broad, with no clear explanation of who it suits and which needs it addresses.

Customers compare you on price alone

Important differences in service, fit or support may be missing, unclear or unsupported by evidence.

Questions keep delaying decisions

Prospects ask about inclusions, timelines, trade-offs, limitations or what happens after they buy.

(02)

Start with the offer you want to clarify

Share the offer, the customers it is for and the questions people ask before choosing. We can discuss a focused review of how it is described, structured and evaluated.

This service focuses on the offer itself. It does not automatically include redesigning a website, setting prices, negotiating sales, launching campaigns or delivering an ongoing optimization program.

(03)

How an offer improvement project can work

We start with the decision a potential customer is trying to make. These four stages are a proposed approach; research access, review methods and responsibilities are agreed before work begins.

  1. 01

    Clarify the customer and the problem

    Identify who the offer should help, the situation they are facing and what they need to understand before buying. Review approved business materials and available questions or feedback; mark missing customer evidence as an open assumption.

  2. 02

    Review the promise and supporting evidence

    Check what is included, the practical benefit, meaningful differences and the proof available for each claim. Separate verified business facts from aspirations, marketing language and features that still need validation.

  3. 03

    Develop clearer offer options

    Explore ways to explain or organize the offer: simpler wording, an explicit scope, suitable package choices or clearer comparisons. Document trade-offs, delivery constraints and pricing assumptions rather than presenting an unapproved option as available.

  4. 04

    Plan what to validate before launch

    Choose the key questions to test, relevant customer feedback or available evidence to review, and the decision each finding would support. Define who approves the options and any later website, proposal or sales-material changes.

(04)

Make the value specific, not exaggerated

A useful value proposition says whom the offer helps, which problem it addresses, what the customer receives and why those details matter. It should not promise an outcome the business cannot substantiate or supply. Real differences matter more than unsupported superlatives.

(05)

What you can receive

Two proposed outputs connect the offer explanation with a practical next decision. Their depth, format and any supporting research depend on the agreed brief.

Value Proposition and Offer Options

A clearer statement of the intended customer, the problem, the relevant benefit and what the business can actually provide. It may include proposed headlines, scope explanations, inclusion and exclusion lists, and ways to compare offer choices.

Options can show important trade-offs: what each choice includes, who it is suited to, its prerequisites and which differences are supported by approved evidence. Proposed packages or commercial terms are not published until the owner confirms delivery feasibility and pricing.

Validation Plan

A structured list of the highest-impact questions to check before adopting a revised offer. It identifies assumptions, possible sources of feedback, review owners, decision criteria and a suitable way to record what is learned.

The plan may suggest reviewing inquiry questions, holding permitted customer conversations or comparing draft messages. Those activities are not assumed to have happened; recruiting, research, tests and implementation require an agreed scope.

(06)

Implementation and measurement boundaries

The brief separates analysis and proposed copy from package approval, pricing decisions, creative production, publishing and customer testing. Changes to contracts, sales tools, website pages or paid campaigns require their own authorized responsibilities.

Any improvement in inquiries, sales or customer understanding must be assessed after a change using suitable evidence. A clearer description can support a decision, but it does not guarantee purchases or revenue.

(07)

Illustrative offer clarification example

The following situation is fictional. It illustrates a possible approach, not completed client work or proven business results.

A service business has one broad package and many repeated questions

Imagine a business whose website says it provides an all-in-one solution. Prospects ask what is included, whether the service fits smaller projects and how it differs from alternatives. The team answers these questions individually because the published offer is too general.

A focused review could list the questions customers ask, compare them with the current offer description and identify benefits the business can support. It might propose one clearly defined core package, a separate option for more complex needs and a comparison of inclusions and exclusions.

The Value Proposition and Offer Options would explain those choices as proposals. The Validation Plan would identify which assumptions need customer or operational checks before any package or price is approved. No outcomes are implied.

(08)

What we may need from you

Share current offer pages, brochures or proposals, your intended customers, actual service inclusions and exclusions, common objections and approved proof. Provide price, delivery or margin information only through agreed secure channels when needed.

(09)

What we agree before starting

Customer and offer

Identify the buyers, current offer, most important questions and decisions to support.

Evidence and constraints

Confirm approved facts, what can be delivered, the differences that can be supported and any commercial information that needs protection.

Outputs and review

Agree the value proposition, offer options, validation questions, approvers and whether actual feedback collection or tests are included.

Terms and next steps

Define responsibilities, revision rounds, ownership, approval gates, price, schedule and any separately scoped publication or sales-material work.

(10)

Questions about improving your offer

What is a value proposition?

It explains whom an offer serves, what problem it addresses, the practical value it provides and why the proposed choice may suit that customer.

Do we need to change our prices?

Not necessarily. The review may show that clearer scope or packaging is more useful. Any pricing recommendation needs sound commercial inputs and owner approval.

Can you help if we offer several services?

Yes. We can discuss a defined set of offers and how their audiences, inclusions and differences should be explained. The agreed scope sets the number reviewed.

Does this include new website pages or sales materials?

Not automatically. This service can define approved messages and offer options. Writing, designing or publishing new assets is included only when agreed.

How do we know which proposed option is better?

The Validation Plan identifies questions, suitable evidence and decision criteria. Any interviews, tests or rollout must be separately approved and carried out.

Will clearer offers guarantee more sales?

No. Customer decisions also depend on need, timing, trust, price, alternatives and delivery. We can recommend clearer choices and ways to evaluate them, not promise sales.
